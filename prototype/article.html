Go

Go is an abstract strategy board game for two players. It was invented in China more than 2,500 years ago and is believed to be the oldest board game continuously played to the present day. Timeline

Selected events in the history of the game.

  1. 1612The Go academy
    The Tokugawa shogunate patronises the game and forms the houses of professional players; Honinbo Sansa is appointed head.
  2. c. 1651Huang Longshi
    Huang Longshi (c. 1650 – c. 1694), one of the strongest players in history, is born.
    Huang Longshi
  3. 1846The Ear-Reddening Game
    Shusaku's move 127 stuns Gennan Inseki, and becomes one of the best moves of all time.
  4. March 2016AlphaGo defeats Lee Sedol
    DeepMind's AlphaGo wins four games to one against Lee Sedol in Seoul.

Timeline of historic inventions (a bulleted list today)

  1. 3.3 Mya – 1 Mya
    1. 3.3 Mya – 2.6 MyaStone tools
      Found in modern-day Kenya are older and only found on the archetype road. Ancient stone tools from Ethiopia (Oldowan) were hand-crafted by Australopithecus or related people.
    2. 1.76 MyaAdvanced (Acheulean) stone tools
      In Kenya by Homo erectus.
    3. 1.75 Mya – 150 kyaOrigin of language
      Varying estimates.
    4. 1.5 MyaControl of fire
      Earliest likely, by Homo erectus.
    5. 1.5 MyaBone tools
      In Africa by Homo erectus and/or Paranthropus boisei.
  2. 1 Mya and later
    1. 1 Mya – 780 kyaCooking
      In the Middle East by Homo erectus.
    2. 900 kya – 40 kyaRafts
    3. 500 kyaHafting
      In South Africa by Homo heidelbergensis.
    4. 500 kya – 450 kyaWoodworking construction
      In Zambia by Homo heidelbergensis. (The oldest known surviving buildings are made from stone and date back no more than 9,500 years.)
    5. 420 kya – 200 kyaFood storage
      In the form of uncracked bones saved for their marrow in Qesem cave, Israel.
    6. 400 kyaHide and leather
      Clothing, footwear and shelter.
    7. 400 kyaPigments
      In Zambia by Homo heidelbergensis.
    8. 337 kya – 300 kyaSchöningen spears
      In Germany, likely by Homo heidelbergensis or early Neanderthals.
    9. 320 kyaTrade
      Long-distance (up to 50 miles) transportation of resources (e.g. obsidian), use of pigments, and possible making of projectile points in Kenya.
